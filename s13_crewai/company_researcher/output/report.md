# Pestana Hotel Group: Wholesale Partner Assessment for Sara’s Candle Shop

## Verdict: **Maybe**

Pestana Hotel Group looks like a **credible and potentially good wholesale prospect**, but it is **not an easy first win**. It is large enough to offer real volume and recurring reorders, yet big hotel groups usually have stricter procurement rules, supplier approval processes, and local buying decisions. For Sara’s small candle business, Pestana is worth pursuing **if she is ready to sell a polished, hospitality-friendly product and start small**.

## Opportunity

Pestana could be attractive for Sara because it is a major hospitality group with:

- **More than 100 hotels**
- **More than 12,000 rooms**
- Presence in **17 countries**
- Brands that include hotels, heritage properties, and lifestyle hotels

That scale means a successful listing could lead to **repeat orders** across multiple properties. Candles also fit well with Pestana’s focus on **guest experience**, **memorable stays**, and **sustainability** through its **Planet Guest** program. A hand-poured scented candle from a small brand could appeal as a way to add atmosphere in:

- guest rooms
- lobbies
- spa areas
- welcome amenities
- boutique or heritage properties

If Sara can position her candles as **premium, locally made, and aligned with ESG/local community values**, Pestana’s brand story could fit her product nicely.

## Risks

There are some real risks to keep in mind:

1. **Large-company complexity**  
   Pestana is a multinational group, so Sara may face approval layers, long lead times, and strict vendor requirements.

2. **Local buying decisions**  
   Pestana’s help center says some services are handled **locally by hotel and region**, which suggests the best entry point may be **one property or one region**, not the whole chain.

3. **Price and consistency pressure**  
   Hotels usually need reliable replenishment, strong margins, and consistent product quality. A small candle maker must prove she can deliver the same scent, packaging, and burn quality every time.

4. **Operational requirements**  
   Hotel accounts may require:
   - insurance
   - safety documentation
   - fragrance and labeling compliance
   - stable production capacity
   - clear lead times

5. **Not all hotels will want candles**  
   Some properties may prefer diffusers, amenities, or no scented products due to brand standards, allergy concerns, or housekeeping policies.

## First step Sara should take

Sara should **target one Pestana property or one local regional contact first**, not the whole group.

A smart first step would be to prepare a **short hospitality sample pack** and outreach message that includes:

- 2–3 candle SKUs only
- a clean wholesale price list
- scent descriptions written for hotel guests
- burn time, ingredients, and packaging details
- proof of small-batch quality and consistency
- one low-risk trial offer for a **pilot order**

She should aim to get a conversation with a **single hotel manager, procurement contact, or local guest experience team** and ask whether they would consider a **trial placement** in guest rooms, reception, or a welcome amenity program.

## Bottom line

**Pestana is a worthwhile prospect, but not a simple wholesale account.**  
For Sara, the opportunity is real because Pestana is large, premium, and experience-focused. The risk is that it is also a sophisticated buyer with local variation and higher operational expectations. The best approach is a **small pilot with one property**, then expand if the trial works.
